## 1. The month in one paragraph

Markets went from "buy everything AI" to questioning AI's returns. A sharp selloff in semiconductors and AI-infrastructure stocks began in Korea on 13 October and spread worldwide. It hit neoclouds (specialised AI compute-rental firms), data-centre names and anything financed with expensive debt. Several pressures drove it at once:
- **Inflation and rates:** US inflation re-accelerated (September CPI +0.6% m/m, gasoline +9.1%) and bond yields hit multi-decade highs.
- **Oil:** Brent climbed back above USD 110 as attacks on Gulf energy infrastructure intensified.

Bonds rallied on safe-haven demand during the worst days. UBS's view: earnings and AI demand remain strong, high-quality bonds now pay enough to cushion further rate rises, and geopolitics alone is no reason to turn defensive.

**UBS on bonds:** fixed income remains **Attractive**.
- **Breakeven yield rises:** US Treasury yields would need to rise about 254 bp (2-year), 113 bp (5-year) or 65 bp (10-year) within a year before price losses wipe out the income.
- **Maturities:** UBS prefers medium-to-long maturities but stays cautious on the very long end, given fiscal deficits and heavy issuance.

## 3. Energy and geopolitics

- **Oil:** Brent rose from a June low of about USD 72 to about **USD 110–113**, the highest since May and the third straight weekly gain.
  - **What drove it:** more Iranian attacks on Hormuz shipping (26 in 1–18 Oct vs 14 in all of September), plus attacks on a Saudi Aramco field and on Saudi airports.
  - **Diesel:** prices remain above 2022 levels.
- **UBS base case:** prices stay high near term, then ease to **about USD 90 in H1 2027**, as OPEC output recovers (+0.92 mb/d in September), pipelines bypass Hormuz and other regions produce more. A diplomatic deal would bring prices down faster.
- **Bloomberg's geopolitical themes:**
  - Iran's missile and drone factories reportedly survived months of bombing.
  - US officials suggest the Iran war could last another year.
  - A Trump–Putin diesel deal was widely criticised, and the Trump–Zelenskyy rift widened.
  - Germany's Chancellor warned that Russia's ambitions go beyond Ukraine.
- **Implication (UBS):** broad commodities, not just oil, are recommended as a portfolio diversifier and hedge against supply shocks. Copper is UBS's preferred commodity.

## 4. Equities and the AI trade

**What happened (Bloomberg):**
- **Korea:** chip stocks crashed after a CEO warned of a "casino"-like market. The KOSPI fell about 6% on 13 October and about 5.7% on 15 October.
- **AI infrastructure:**
  - CoreWeave lost more than 20% in a week as its debt sold off.
  - Data-centre firm Elice fell 32% on its IPO debut.
  - Nextdc fell up to 44% after cutting its outlook.
  - SoftBank's market value has halved since June.
- **Positioning:**
  - Short sellers made an estimated USD 32bn from the chip rout.
  - Hedge-fund bearish bets are near a five-year high, with prime-brokerage clients shorting about five times as much as they buy.
  - A prominent hedge-fund manager called AI "almost certainly" a bubble.
- **Tone:** Bloomberg's framing is that investors now demand proof of AI returns, and the "buy everything AI" phase is over.
- **Other notable moves:**
  - China stocks were on the verge of a bear market, down about 20% from the June high.
  - **Indonesia** saw its worst rout in about 30 years after MSCI warned it could downgrade the market.
  - Bank of America fell about 7% on fines and weak trading.
  - GM cut its outlook.
  - Tesla and SpaceX are studying a combination.

**UBS view:**
- **Earnings:** UBS expects S&P 500 earnings per share to grow about **28% y/y** in the third quarter, with semiconductors contributing about half. AI capex is about USD 900bn this year, and cloud revenue growth should accelerate to about 58% y/y.
- **Targets:** S&P 500 at **8,400 by June 2027**. Eurozone earnings growth about 15% in both 2026 and 2027.
- **AI stance:** stay invested but be **selective**.
  - **Limit neoclouds,** given funding and construction risks.
  - **Favour well-funded hyperscalers** (the large cloud providers) and high-quality semiconductor and hardware names.
  - **Add defensive tech:** payment networks, select software.
- **Asia ex-Japan:** valuation is about **9× forward P/E**, a multi-decade low. UBS forecasts earnings growth of about 72% in 2026 and 20% in 2027, roughly two-thirds driven by AI.
- **Favourites:** Taiwan, India, China A-shares, Singapore and Japan. In the US: financials, industrials, utilities, health care and consumer discretionary. Also Eurozone equities. Themes: **AI, Power & resources, Longevity**.

## 6. Takeaways I'm watching

1. **AI dispersion matters more than AI exposure.** The selloff punished highly indebted, single-product AI infrastructure names much more than cash-rich platforms and chip leaders.
2. **Bonds are back as a hedge.** Treasuries rallied during the equity rout, and with yields this high, income cushions further rises.
3. **Energy is the main macro risk.** It feeds inflation and keeps central banks hawkish. UBS sees broad commodities as a portfolio diversifier.
4. **Asia is cheap but volatile.** UBS is constructive at a 9× P/E but selective: Taiwan, India and Singapore over Indonesia and Malaysia. Korea and China saw the sharpest swings.
5. **Watch list:**
   - US midterms (3 Nov).
   - Fed meetings, December in particular.
   - Further Gulf attacks.
   - French spreads.
   - Q3 AI-capex commentary from hyperscalers.
