## 1. Summary

The period was dominated by a selloff in semiconductors and AI-infrastructure stocks that began in Korea on 13 October and spread globally, set against re-accelerating US inflation, multi-decade-high Treasury yields, and higher oil on Middle East supply disruption. Bonds rallied on safe-haven demand during the worst sessions. UBS's position through the batch was unchanged: AI demand remains robust, high-quality bonds now offer enough income to cushion further rate rises, and geopolitics alone is not a reason to turn defensive. The newest items (23–26 October) add a late-week AI relief attempt that failed to steady sentiment, a widening in credit spreads, and Lula's election win in Brazil.

## 3. Energy and geopolitics

- **Oil:** Brent rose over the batch from about **USD 93** (22 Sep) to **USD 107–117**, touching **USD 110** on 17 October (first since May). WTI was around USD 92–95 in mid-October.
  - **Drivers (Bloomberg):** intensified Iranian attacks on Hormuz shipping; Houthi strikes on Saudi airports (at least 14 killed on 12 October); a ballistic missile intercepted near the Saudi Red Sea oil outlet of Yanbu; a failed Russia–Ukraine "energy ceasefire," after which Ukraine struck the Ryazan refinery; a worsening global tanker shortage.
  - **Supply response:** the G7 released 100 million barrels of emergency stocks via the IEA; OPEC output rose about 1.78 mb/d in September to 21.49 mb/d (Reuters survey), still below quotas.
  - **UBS view:** prices stay elevated near term then ease; UBS **raised its year-end Brent forecast to about USD 100**. It continues to recommend broad commodities as a diversifier.
- **Politics (Bloomberg):**
  - Trump's weekend diesel deal with Putin drew bipartisan criticism; the Trump–Zelenskyy rift widened, with Zelenskyy saying Trump was pressuring Kyiv to yield.
  - Germany's Chancellor Merz warned Russia's aims extend beyond Ukraine.
  - The administration moved to suspend a visa program used by technology firms (Microsoft, Adobe), narrowing access to foreign talent.
  - **Brazil (26 Oct):** Lula won a record fourth term over Flávio Bolsonaro; Bloomberg reported Brazilian markets were bracing for losses after the investor-favoured candidate lost, and that Bolsonaro was seeking annulment.

## 4. Equities and the AI trade

**What happened (Bloomberg):**
- **Korea:** chip stocks fell sharply after a CEO's "casino" warning — the KOSPI dropped about 6% on 13 October and about 5.7% on 15 October.
- **AI infrastructure:** CoreWeave lost more than 20% in a week with its bonds at distressed levels; data-centre firm Elice fell 32% on its Seoul debut; the chip benchmark extended losses to an eighth straight session (its longest streak in about two decades).
- **Positioning:** short sellers made an estimated **USD 32 billion** from the chip rout; by 23 October global credit spreads were at a 17-month high and hedge funds recorded their widest daily drawdown since the pandemic. A late-week recovery attempt on 23 October did not steady sentiment.
- **Fundraising:** OpenAI reset a USD 30 billion round at a USD 1.25 trillion valuation; Anthropic was reported targeting roughly a USD 2 trillion IPO valuation and said quarterly revenue reached USD 16.8 billion (adjusted-profitable).
- **Other moves:** Indonesia saw its worst rout in about 30 years (an 8% drop and trading halt on 15 October, lowest since 2020 by 19 October) after MSCI flagged a possible downgrade; Bank of America fell about 7% on a USD 1.2 billion penalty and weak trading; GM fell up to 7.5% on a narrower outlook despite a USD 20 billion buyback; the Indian rupee rose 1% after RBI intervention. Blackstone's Jon Gray reiterated a broad AI commitment.

**UBS view (Daily Asia, 8–26 Oct):**
- **AI demand "robust":** TSMC reported +51% quarterly revenue; Samsung flagged a large operating-profit jump; GPU rental prices rose (four-year-old GPUs +7% m/m, ~30% YTD; two-year-old +35% YTD); cloud revenue growth is seen accelerating from 48% to **58% y/y** in Q3. UBS estimates **AI capex +33% to USD 1.2 trillion in 2027**, with neoclouds under 10% of that.
- **Stance:** stay invested but **selective** — **favour well-funded hyperscalers** (estimated cost of debt 6.5–7%, ROIC ~25%+) and **limit exposure to neoclouds**, whose single-product model carries more funding and execution risk.
- **Earnings:** UBS expects **Q3 S&P 500 EPS +28% y/y**, with semiconductors contributing about half and breadth beyond tech (nine straight months of ISM new-orders expansion; +6% y/y September retail sales).
- **FX note:** UBS called the euro's five-week slide against the dollar "overdone."

## 6. Takeaways I'm watching

1. **Dispersion within AI.** The selloff hit highly indebted, single-product infrastructure names (neoclouds) much harder than cash-rich platforms and chip leaders — the UBS and Bloomberg readings agree on that split even where their conclusions differ.
2. **Bonds as a hedge again.** Treasuries rallied on the worst equity days, and at current yields the income cushions further rate rises.
3. **Energy is the main macro risk.** It feeds inflation and keeps central banks hawkish; UBS still points to broad commodities as a diversifier.
4. **Policy gap.** Markets lean toward a Fed hold; UBS expects hikes in December and March. The December meeting is the key test.
5. **New this batch:** credit spreads at a 17-month high, Brazil's election result, and the visa move against tech employers.
