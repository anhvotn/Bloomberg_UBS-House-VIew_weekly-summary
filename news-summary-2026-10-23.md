## The through-line

Bloomberg's briefings capture a market losing its nerve — yields at multi-decade highs, oil surging on Middle East attacks, and the AI trade flipping from euphoria to a demand for proof of returns (neoclouds, SoftBank and China all cracking, hedge funds at bearish extremes). UBS looks at the same picture and reaches the opposite conclusion: higher rates, firmer energy and AI jitters warrant *selectivity and diversification*, not retreat — it keeps equities, high-quality bonds and commodities all Attractive, leaning on ~28% earnings growth and quality names (hyperscalers over neoclouds) while trimming the concentrated single-stock and lower-quality AI exposure that Bloomberg's headlines are punishing.

## UBS House View

UBS's message is strikingly against the grain of the Bloomberg mood: markets face "higher hurdles," but UBS is **not** turning defensive.

### Monthly Letter — "Divided" (Oct 22)

The flagship view. Four pillars: earnings resilience, bond repricing, energy risks, "beyond midterms."

- **Equities Attractive.** Q3 S&P 500 EPS seen **+28% y/y** (~half from semis). AI capex ~$900B this year. **S&P 500 target 8,400 by end-June 2027.** Likes financials, industrials, utilities, health care, consumer discretionary in the US; Eurozone; Japan, India, Taiwan, Singapore in Asia. Favors well-funded hyperscalers over neoclouds.
- **Bonds Attractive after repricing.** US 10y hit 5.36% in early October (highest since 2002), now ~5.1%. UBS now expects the **Fed to hike in both December and March**, but says current yields offer enough income to cushion further rises (2y yields would need to jump ~254bps to wipe out a year's income). Favors medium/long duration; cautious on the longest maturities.
- **Energy:** Brent ~$110 (from $72 in June); base case moderation to ~$90 in H1 2027. A risk scenario (gasoline $170, diesel $250) would slow US growth toward low-1% but **not** cause recession. Does not justify a defensive stance.
- **Midterms:** 90% chance of divided government; UBS says don't treat it as a binary market event — earnings/AI/rates matter more.

### CIO Monthly Extended (Nov, pub. Oct 22)

Full asset-allocation detail.

- **Everything Attractive:** global/US/Europe/Switzerland/UK/EM/Japan/Asia-ex-Japan equities; overall fixed income (HG, IG, HY, EM); commodities (special focus **copper**).
- **FX:** GBP, NOK, CNY Attractive; USD, EUR, CHF, JPY, CAD, NZD, AUD Neutral. Expects USD to weaken; EURUSD year-end target **1.16**. Notable: Fed, ECB and BoJ all raised rates in September — a historical first.
- **Scenarios to June 2027:** Base "Resilient growth" **50%** (S&P 8,400, +8%); Bull "Goldilocks" 25% (S&P 9,500); Bear I "Yield reset" 15% (S&P 7,000); Bear II "AI bear market" 10% (S&P 5,500). Gold base case **$5,200/oz**.

### Equity Compass — "Steady under pressure" (Oct 22)

**Rating changes: downgraded Indonesia to Unattractive (from Neutral)** on MSCI index-change proposals and rising discount rates, and **downgraded Malaysia to Neutral** on political uncertainty ahead of its general election. Reiterates Attractive US/Europe/Asia, AI/Power & resources/Longevity themes.

### Investing in APAC — "Clearing higher hurdles" (Nov)

Asia ex-Japan at ~9x forward P/E (near multi-decade low); forecasts **+72% EPS growth 2026, +20% 2027** (AI ~two-thirds of it). Preferred: **Taiwan, India, onshore China A-shares, Singapore**, and shorter-duration IG bonds. Confirms the Malaysia/Indonesia downgrades.

### Daily Asia (Oct 13, 22, 23)

Day-to-day echoes of the above: stay invested through geopolitical drawdowns (S&P higher 12 months after ~¾ of crises since 1941); use structured strategies (put warrants, capital-preservation, reverse convertibles) and hedge funds to build resilience; commodities for diversification. The Oct 23 note flagged China's technical bear market but called it positioning/sentiment-driven, not a fundamentals breakdown — favoring A-shares over H-shares.
